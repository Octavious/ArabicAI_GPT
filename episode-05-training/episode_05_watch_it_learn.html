Reading the loss curve

The curve has the shape every healthy training run shares: a steep drop early, then a long, slow decline. In the first 1,000 steps the model goes from "completely random" to "knows basic letter and word patterns" — that's the cliff at the left. After that, every further improvement is harder won.

The two lines are training loss (orange) and validation loss (blue). They stay close together the whole run — that's the sign of healthy learning. If the blue line had peeled away and climbed while orange kept falling, that would be overfitting: the model memorizing its training data instead of learning general patterns.

What is perplexity?

Loss is precise but not intuitive. Perplexity is the same information in a form you can feel: it's roughly "how many equally-likely options is the model choosing between?" Perplexity is just e^loss.

At step 0, loss is 8.32 and perplexity is 4,096 — the model is choosing uniformly among all 4,096 vocabulary tokens. It has no idea. By step 25,000, loss is ~1.7 and perplexity is around 5–6 — the model has narrowed "what comes next" down to a small handful of sensible options. That's the whole story of training, in one number.

A perplexity of 5 means the model is about as uncertain as if it were picking from 5 equally likely words — a dramatic improvement from 4,096, and good enough to produce coherent text.

The five stages of learning to write

Scrub slowly through the run and the output passes through five recognizable stages. They always appear in this order — it's the same progression every language model goes through, from the smallest to the largest.

Noise → Letters → Words → Grammar → Stories. First random tokens. Then plausible letter clusters and the most common short words. Then real words in roughly the right order. Then mostly-correct grammar and sentence structure. Finally, coherent short narratives with characters and events. The model is never told these stages — they emerge on their own, as a natural consequence of getting better at "predict the next token."
